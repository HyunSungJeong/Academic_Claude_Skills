\section*{Summary}

\file{pdf-to-markdown} is a Claude skill that converts PDFs of physics papers,
notes and textbooks into Markdown with LaTeX mathematics. Every formula is
transcribed from page images rather than from the PDF's text layer, then checked
mechanically and proofread against the original.

Version 2.2 makes the skill cheaper to run without changing how formulas are
read: page strips are rendered at 170\,dpi instead of 300\,dpi, the text layer is
no longer read page by page, every token of output is written once, and the
preparation script prints everything the transcription needs on one line per
page. On the five-page test note this is estimated to cut a run from about
45{,}000 to about 30{,}000 tokens. The lower resolution was adopted only after a
blind legibility test and an open blind transcription of two unseen pages, both
without a single error (Section~\ref{sec:tests}).

The evidence still comes from born-digital, single-column documents only. The
known limitations in Section~\ref{sec:limits}, in particular scanned PDFs and
two-column layouts, should be read before relying on the skill elsewhere.

\section{Why version 1.0 was replaced}

Version~1.0 was run on the test document and compared with its LaTeX source.
The text layer, through markitdown or otherwise, proved unusable for mathematics:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Invented tables.} markitdown 0.1.5 split ordinary sentences on page~2
  into 60 rows of Markdown table cells.
\item \textbf{Lost typefaces.} Calligraphic $\mathcal G$ and roman $\mathrm G$ both
  came out as the same letter G, and bold $\bm\omega$ as a plain $\omega$. The test note's
  notation rests on exactly this distinction.
\item \textbf{Detached accents.} $\tilde{\mathrm K}=1/\Omega_{\bar 1}$ was extracted
  as \texttt{K = 1 \~{} /}$\Omega$\texttt{\textasciimacron 1}, the tilde moved off the K and next to the 1.
\item \textbf{Placeholder glyphs.} Integral and sum signs and brace pieces leaked
  as \verb|(cid:90)|, \verb|(cid:88)| and similar codes.
\item \textbf{Useless page flagging.} The Unicode-count rule flagged all five
  pages, since every page of a physics note contains mathematics.
\item \textbf{Broken paragraphs.} Page markers landed in the middle of sentences
  that continue across a page break.
\end{itemize}

\section{What version 2 does}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
\toprule

  writing conventions, context budget. \\
\file{scripts/prepare.py} & Per page: 170\,dpi strips cut in the gaps between
  lines, a text layer for searching single words, the share of characters set in
  math fonts, and the equation numbers printed at the right margin. Prints one
  line per page: image or text, each strip with its $y$-range in points, tags. \\
\file{scripts/assemble.py} & Joins the per-page Markdown files in page order;
  rejoins a paragraph that a page break interrupted. \\
\file{scripts/check.py} & Every formula must render in KaTeX; every printed
  equation number must appear exactly once as \texttt{\textbackslash tag\{\}}; no
  text-layer debris. \\
\file{scripts/verify\_eqs.py} & Retypesets each numbered display equation with
  pdflatex and places it under the PDF's original, four equations per sheet. \\
\file{scripts/eval/mathcmp.py} & Formula-by-formula comparison with a LaTeX
  source; works on any tool's Markdown (testing only). \\
\file{scripts/eval/prosecmp.py} & Word-by-word prose comparison with a LaTeX
  source (testing only). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Writing conventions enforced by \file{SKILL.md}: typefaces reproduced exactly
($\mathcal K$, $\mathrm K$, $\bm\Omega$, upright $\mathrm i$, $\mathrm d$,
$\mathrm e$ where printed so); printed equation numbers used in
\texttt{\textbackslash tag\{\}}, even when out of sequence; zoomed crops at
500--600\,dpi before deciding any multi-character bar such as $\overline{j+1}$
versus $\bar{j}+1$, any tilde, hat or prime, and the extent of every brace.

\section{What changed in 2.2}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strips at 170\,dpi.} \file{prepare.py} now renders strips at
  170\,dpi by default. At 300\,dpi a strip was about 2{,}000\,px wide and was
  downscaled by the model to an effective 232\,dpi anyway; at 170\,dpi it is about
  1{,}100\,px wide and is seen as rendered. Strip tokens fall by about 46\%.
\item \textbf{No reading of the text layer.} \file{SKILL.md} forbids reading
  \file{page\_NNN.txt} as a whole; it may only be searched for one doubtful word.
  Prose read from the strips was error-free in every test.
\item \textbf{Every token of output written once.} Each page is written with
  \cmd{create\_file} to its own \file{page\_NNN.md}. A page ending mid-paragraph
  ends with the line \texttt{<!-- continues -->}. \file{assemble.py} joins the
  pages and rejoins such paragraphs; corrections after checking and proofreading
  are targeted \cmd{str\_replace} edits. The document is never re-typed.
\item \textbf{Compact script output.} \file{prepare.py} prints one line per page
  with the strip files, their $y$-ranges in points (for zooms) and the equation
  tags, so \file{manifest.json} never needs to be opened.
\item \textbf{Proofreading printout.} Step~5 now requires reading the full output
  of \file{verify\_eqs.py} first: a \texttt{PROBLEM} line marks an equation that
  could not be typeset and is therefore missing from the sheets. This is an
  instruction only; the script itself is unchanged.
\item \textbf{Documentation.} \cmd{--lenient}, exact-count reading of scores and
  scoring other tools' output are now described in \file{SKILL.md}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Test results}
\label{sec:tests}

Two documents with their LaTeX sources were available as ground truth: a
five-page note on Eq.~(40) of Kugler, Lee \& von Delft (``Kugler note'') and a
ten-page note on particle--vortex duality (``duality note''). Both are
single-column and born-digital (pdf\TeX). All transcriptions were made from the
images before the source was opened.

\subsection*{Accuracy on the Kugler note}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Lrr@{}}
\toprule
Measure & Whole document & Page 3 \\
\midrule
Displayed equations exactly correct & 25 / 26 & 8 / 8 \\
Inline formulas exactly correct & 143 / 147 & 38 / 38 \\
Prose & identical & 162 / 162 words \\
\file{check.py} (KaTeX, tags, debris) & pass & pass \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The five whole-document mismatches render identically to the source:
\verb|F_{\rm rest}| versus \verb|F_{\mathrm{rest}}|, the arrow written as the
Unicode character U+21D2 instead of \verb|$\Rightarrow$|, parentheses outside
rather than inside \verb|$...$|, and ``K-part'' written as text. With the 2.2
pipeline (per-page files, \file{assemble.py}, \file{check.py},
\file{verify\_eqs.py}) the assembled document was identical to the original
transcription and scored the same.

\subsection*{Blind legibility test (basis for 170\,dpi)}

Sixteen items, each with two or three near-identical variants (for example
$\omega'$ versus $\omega$, a bar over $j+1$ versus over $j$ alone, $\mathcal K$
versus $\mathrm K$, $\tilde{\mathrm K}$ versus $\hat{\mathrm K}$, $+$ versus $-$ in
an exponent, swapped indices) were typeset in the note's fonts. A script drew one
variant per item at random for each resolution and kept the key hidden until all
readings were recorded.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Lrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Effective resolution (dpi) & 232 & 200 & 170 & 145 & 120 & 100 \\
\midrule
Image tokens relative to 232\,dpi & 100\% & 74\% & 54\% & 39\% & 27\% & 19\% \\
Items read correctly & 16/16 & 16/16 & 16/16 & 16/16 & 16/16 & 16/16 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The test is forced-choice (the candidate variants were known), which is easier
than open transcription; at 120 and 100\,dpi about five readings felt uncertain,
at 145\,dpi and above none did except one overbar extent. 170\,dpi was chosen to
keep a wide margin.

\subsection*{Open blind transcription at 170\,dpi}

Pages 4 and 6 of the duality note, chosen as the two pages with the most
math-font characters, were transcribed from 170\,dpi strips without zooms. Their
hazards include tildes on $\phi$ and $f_{\mu\nu}$, daggers, integral limits
$\int_x^\infty$ versus $\int_\infty^x$, signs in exponents and a primed $n'$.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Page & Displayed equations & Inline formulas & Prose \\
\midrule
4 & 4/4 & 34/34 & identical \\
6 & 6/6 & 36/36 & identical \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

All matches are strict (no \cmd{--lenient}). Caveat: the prose of these pages
was already known from the text layer; only the mathematics was unseen.

\subsection*{Proofreading test}

Errors of random type were planted, with a hidden key, in random equations of a
copy of the Kugler transcription; the sheets from \file{verify\_eqs.py} were then
reviewed cold.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Round & Planted & Caught on sheets & Caught by compile/KaTeX & Missed \\
\midrule
1 & 1 & 1 & -- & 0 \\
2 & 8 & 5 & 3 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Errors caught on the sheets were a dropped prime, three dropped bolds, a dropped
bar and $\mathcal K$ turned roman; there were no false alarms. Three planted
errors produced LaTeX that does not compile, so their equations never reached
the sheets; the verifier reported them, but only in its text output, which led
to the new instruction in step~5. Limits: nine errors in total, no sign flips
or index swaps among them, and compile failures are partly an artifact of how
the errors were planted.

\textbf{Scorer validation.} Four realistic errors planted in the page-3
transcription were all flagged at the right places, while spelling-only variants
still scored 8/8 and 38/38. With the errors present the mean token similarity
stayed at 0.993 and 0.999, which is why only exact counts are meaningful.

\textbf{Not yet tested against Marker.} A page-3 comparison kit is provided
separately (\file{page3\_comparison\_kit.zip}); Marker's model weights are hosted
on \file{models.datalab.to} and \file{huggingface.co}, which Claude's sandbox
cannot reach, so the kit must be run locally.

\section{Changes considered and not made}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Selective proofreading (rejected).} Skipping ``simple'' equations
  would save almost nothing: a classifier skipping only equations without bars,
  primes, bold, typeface-sensitive letters, accents or braces skips 1 of 26 in
  the Kugler note. Meanwhile the proofreading test found errors in the plainest
  equations, such as $\mathcal G(\mathrm i\bm\omega)=0$.
\item \textbf{Skipping strips without math fonts (deferred).} No strip of the
  Kugler note and 4 of 39 strips of the duality note (6\% of strip tokens)
  contain no math-font character; all four were genuinely free of mathematics.
  8 of the duality note's 195 inline formulas are set entirely in text fonts
  (\verb|+|, \verb|+1|, \verb|=|) and are invisible to the detector; here none fell
  in a skippable strip. The change needs scan detection and hyphen repair of
  text-layer prose first, and the benefit is small.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scanned PDFs (serious).} A scan has no fonts, so \file{prepare.py}
  reports every page as \texttt{text-ok}. Until fixed, scanned pages must be
  transcribed from images regardless of what the printout says.
\item \textbf{Two-column layouts.} Equation numbers are detected only in the right
  quarter of the page, so left-column equations are missed by the tag checklist;
  proofreading crops span the full page width and include the other column.
\item \textbf{Dotted equation numbers.} Tags such as (5.40) are not recognised.
  \file{check.py} then reports a correctly transcribed
  \texttt{\textbackslash tag\{5.40\}} as ``not printed in PDF'' and fails.
\item \textbf{Proofreading sheets.} Equations that do not compile are left off the
  sheets rather than shown as placeholders; adjacent equations of one
  \texttt{align} environment are merged into one crop; large symbols can be
  clipped at the crop edge.
\item \textbf{Fonts.} Math detection matches known math font names. Documents
  from Word, with unusual fonts, or with bitmap (Type~3) math fonts, as in older
  arXiv PDFs, may be misclassified.
\item \textbf{Self-proofreading.} Without a source, the side-by-side check is done
  by the same model that made the transcription. Sheets cover numbered displays
  only; unnumbered displays and inline math rely on the syntax check and on
  re-reading the strips.
\item \textbf{Context budget.} A 170\,dpi strip costs about 600 tokens and a
  proofreading sheet about 1{,}500. A ten-page note fits in one conversation;
  long papers and books should be converted one section or chapter at a time.
\item \textbf{Narrow evidence.} Accuracy results come from seven transcribed
  pages of two documents of the same kind.
\end{itemize}

\section{Installation and requirements}

Upload \file{pdf-to-markdown.zip} in Claude under \emph{Customize $\rightarrow$
Skills} and make sure code execution is enabled. If an earlier version is
installed, replace it. The scripts need PyMuPDF and Pillow; \file{verify\_eqs.py}
needs pdflatex and \file{check.py} needs Node.js, and \file{check.py} installs
KaTeX itself if it is missing. All of these were present in Claude's sandbox
during testing (PyMuPDF 1.28.2).

\section{Planned next}

Accept dotted equation numbers; show uncompilable equations as placeholder
panels and crop \texttt{align} lines and large symbols correctly; treat every
page without a text layer as image-only; detect columns and crop each one
separately; test on a two-column journal page and a scanned textbook page; run
the Marker comparison locally.
